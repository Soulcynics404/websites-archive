Our story

The little cafe at the
heart of Shirley village

It started with a fryer, four tables and a borrowed kettle

In the spring of 1998 we opened the doors on Wickham Road with four tables, one very second-hand fryer and a queue of curious neighbours. The first week we sold out of everything by Wednesday and had to send out for bread.

Twenty-five years on, the tables have doubled and the fryer finally got replaced (twice), but the idea never changed: proper food, cooked in front of you, served by people who know your name.

We still peel our own potatoes for the hash browns, bake every cake downstairs before the sun's properly up, and keep the tea warm for whoever walks in from the rain. That's not a marketing strategy — that's just how a village cafe should be.

Baked downstairs, upstairs by nine

Every morning starts at half five with ovens warming and butter softening. By seven, when the first regular raps on the glass, there are croissants cooling on racks and today's cakes lining the counter: coffee & walnut, lemon drizzle, Victoria sponge, and the fudge cake that has its own fan club.

Nothing sits overnight. What doesn't sell by close goes to the community fridge by the library — because good cake shouldn't go to waste.

"A cafe isn't about the coffee. It's about having somewhere that's pleased to see you."— written on our chalkboard since 1998

What we promise

Three things we'll never budge on

01

Proper ingredients

Eggs from the farm shop down the road, bacon from Smithfield, bread baked overnight in Croydon. No shortcuts hiding in the kitchen.

02

Fair prices

A full breakfast and a pot of tea shouldn't cost a tenner more than it's worth. Our prices work for pensioners, plumbers and families alike.

03

Everyone's welcome

Rush-hour nurses, dog walkers, pram squads and the quiz team after a late one. If Shirley walks through the door, there's a table for them.
