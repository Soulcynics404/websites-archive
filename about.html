La Maison

Built on butter, stock and patience.

The story of a small bistro with old-fashioned standards.

“Fashion changes. A good stock does not.”

Chef-patron Henri Laurent trained for eleven years in Lyon before crossing the channel in 2012 and taking over a tired sandwich shop at the top of Rue de Vieux Marché. He stripped it back to the brickwork, installed a zinc bar from a demolished café in Marseille, and opened with nine tables and one rule: cook properly or don’t bother.

Fourteen years on, the rule holds. Stocks simmer overnight, confit takes two days, and no dish leaves the pass until it would satisfy Henri’s first maître d’. The room fills with regulars who order “the usual” — which is exactly how we like it.
