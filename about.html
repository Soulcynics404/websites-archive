Our story · est. 1959

Sixty-Six Years on the Boulevard

Moby's isn't a theme restaurant pretending to be old — it actually is. Same corner, same grill, same promise: hot coffee and a real breakfast for everyone who walks in.

How it started

A Grill, a Coffee Pot, and a Promise

In 1959, Walter "Moby" Peterson bought a small lunch counter on West Manchester Boulevard with money saved from a decade of short-order cooking. His rule was simple: no cup sits empty, nobody leaves hungry.

His daughter Ruth ran the register for thirty years and added the pie case that made the shop famous. Today her son Marcus runs the morning shift, and you'll still find him cracking eggs at 6 a.m. sharp.

We've watched Inglewood change around us — the Forum days, the Rams coming home, SoFi lighting up the night sky. Through all of it, the booth by the window has stayed exactly where it was in 1959.

  • 1959 — Walter "Moby" Peterson opens a 12-stool counter on W Manchester Blvd. First week's special: two eggs, toast, coffee — 45¢.
  • 1972 — The back dining room is built. The first pie oven arrives; banana cream becomes an instant legend.
  • 1988 — Ruth takes over. She paints the mural of the marlin out front and doubles the breakfast menu.
  • 2004 — Third generation: Marcus joins after culinary school, bringing his grandmother's biscuit recipe to the griddle.
  • 2020 — The neighborhood carries us through takeout-only months. Regulars ordered coffee by phone just to keep us standing. We never forgot that.
  • Today — 900+ Google reviews later, we still open at 6 a.m., every single day.
What we believe

The Moby's Way

Three rules taped inside the kitchen door since day one.

🥓 Real Food, Cooked to Order

Batter mixed each morning, pies baked before sunrise, hash browns grilled when you ask — not when it's convenient for us.

☕ Your Cup Never Empties

The bottomless coffee pot is sacred. If your cup goes dry, that's on us — and the refill is on the house, always has been.

🪑 Everyone Has a Seat

Night-shift nurses, Little League teams, solo readers with the paper — this counter belongs to whoever needs it this morning.
