Born on Well Street,
raised by Hackney

The story of a very small kitchen with a very big table — and the neighbourhood that pulled up a chair.

It started with one espresso machine

We opened Well Street Kitchen with one espresso machine, a four-burner griddle and a simple belief: the best brunch spot in E9 shouldn't feel like a concept — it should feel like someone's kitchen. Ours, ideally.

Day one was quiet. Day two, the lady from the bakery two doors down came in "just to see". By the first weekend there was a queue, and someone had already asked if we did bottomless filter. (We do. Before noon. Always have.)

Grown by the neighbourhood

We've never advertised. Every table of ours exists because someone told their neighbour, their nan, their postman. That's what people mean when they call us a neighbourhood gem — and after 683 Google reviews at 4.7★, we've decided to believe them.

Our eggs come from a farm that delivers before we've unlocked the door. The sourdough arrives warm from the bakery next door. The Well Street market — yards from our window — sorts the rest.

What "modern British" means here

It means a full English that respects tradition but isn't afraid of harissa. It means hotcakes next to kippers. It means halal-friendly plates without a footnote, proper vegan options that aren't afterthoughts, and tea served in a pot big enough for a proper catch-up.

Above all it means generous food, fairly priced, cooked by people who'll probably say hello when you walk back in tomorrow.

"You can taste when a kitchen loves its street. This one adores it."
— The Well Word, local newsletter

Pull up a chair

Five years in, the espresso machine is still the same one (rebuilt twice), the griddle is seasoned with five years of Sundays, and most orders are still known by heart.

Whether it's your first visit or your four-hundredth — the kettle's on, the hotcakes are rising, and there's a table with your name on it. Sort of. We write names on the waitlist now.
