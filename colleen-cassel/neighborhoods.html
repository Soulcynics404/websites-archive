Where to live

Neighborhoods I know cold.

Every guide below is written from walked streets and closed deals — not a tourism site. Tap a card to flip it for my honest read.

Chelsea

Full-service condos by the High Line, classic co-ops on the side streets. Pricey but stable — the galleries keep it interesting. Best for: buyers who want polish plus culture.

Condos · Galleries · Hudson sunsets

West Village

The city's most charming maze — low-rises, leafy blocks, and townhouses that rarely trade. Inventory is thin; patience and relationships win here.

Townhouses · Charm · Low turnover

SoHo & Tribeca

Lofts with columns and light, celebrity-grade privacy downtown. Board minutes run long and price-per-foot runs higher — the ceilings are worth it to some.

Lofts · Privacy · Cast iron

Upper West Side

Park-facing pre-wars with real family layouts and co-op boards that respect stability. Schools, Zabar's, and value per square foot versus the East Side.

Pre-wars · Families · Central Park

Brooklyn Heights

Brownstones, the Promenade, and an easy commute on the 2/3. Historic district rules protect your investment — and slow down renovations. Plan both ways.

Brownstones · Promenade · Historic

Long Island City

Glass towers, one stop from Midtown, and the best amenity-per-dollar equation in the coverage map. Great first-buy territory — watch the tax abatement math.

New builds · Skyline · Value
